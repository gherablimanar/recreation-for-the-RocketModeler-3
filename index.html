ROCKET DESIGN · SIDE VIEW

In Design view, drag the green handles to move fins, the motor assembly and the recovery pack, or resize the nose and body. You can also drag the fins and visible internal parts directly. Use the numeric controls for precise or keyboard edits. All positions are measured along the rocket; the nose stays attached to the body.

Understand the model, markers and actions

CG (yellow) is the center of gravity: the mass balance point. CP (blue) is the center of pressure: the effective point where aerodynamic forces act. Both distances start at the nose tip. They are calculated markers, not movable parts.

Static margin = (CP − CG) / body diameter, measured in calibers (cal). At least 1 cal is labeled stable here; between 0 and 1 is marginal; zero or negative is unstable. Moving a component updates its mass position and these markers immediately.

Design Rocket opens the editor. Launch Rocket opens the flight view. Begin Launch starts a three-second countdown. Pause freezes simulation time; Resume continues. Reset clears the flight while keeping your design. Restart starts a fresh flight. Reset to Default restores all original settings.

Save Design downloads your settings as JSON. Load Design restores a saved JSON design. Export Results downloads flight samples as CSV after a flight starts. Print Validation Run computes a separate complete simulation and displays inputs, equations, events and results.

The five graphs show altitude, speed, acceleration, thrust and mass against elapsed time. Their vertical scales adjust to the recorded maximum. The launch line shows the initial angle, the trajectory shows the traveled path, and the canopy appears during parachute recovery.

Units: m = metres, kg = kilograms, s = seconds, N = newtons of force, N·s = impulse, m/s = speed, m/s² = acceleration. This is a simplified educational simulation with synthetic motor data, not a verified reproduction of NASA flight predictions.
